A traffic signal that gives green time to the busier road

Fixed timers give every road the same time, even when one road is empty. This project tests a simple rule that adjusts green time to the traffic.

The problem

When a signal gives green to an empty road while a busy road queues, time and fuel are wasted. Long idling also adds to emissions and frustration at the junction.

Wasted green

A fixed 15 second green on an empty road is 15 seconds of no movement.

Longer queues

The busy road waits for a cycle that does not match its traffic.

How it works

Sensors on each road measure how often a vehicle is present in a 20 second window. That share of time is called occupancy, a number from 0 to 1.

  1. Measure occupancy on each road.
  2. Set green time: 5 seconds plus 20 seconds times occupancy. So the range is 5 to 25 seconds.
  3. Keep yellow at 3 seconds and all-red at 1 second, fixed for safety.
  4. Repeat for each road in turn.

Try the demo

Change the occupancy for each road, then run one cycle. The lights change in order, about five times faster than real time.

Safety rules

  • Every road always gets a minimum green, so no road is left waiting forever.
  • No road gets more than the maximum green, so one busy road cannot block the others.
  • Yellow and all-red times never change with traffic.
  • Pedestrian crossing time must never be shortened. On a real junction this is checked first.

What you can do

  • Do not block the junction. Wait behind the stop line, even when the light turns green.
  • Switch off the engine when stuck in a long queue, where it is safe to do so.
  • Report a faulty or stuck signal to the official traffic police helpline.
  • Consider public transport or carpooling at peak hours.
Educational prototype. The demo uses toy traffic on a model junction, so it does not represent real Indian roads. Expected benefits are to be measured, not assumed.
